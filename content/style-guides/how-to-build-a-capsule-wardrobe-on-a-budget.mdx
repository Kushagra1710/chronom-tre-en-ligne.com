A capsule wardrobe is useful when it reduces decisions, not when it forces your style into somebody else’s checklist. The goal is a smaller group of clothes that fits your routine and combines easily.

## Begin with your calendar

Write down what a normal month contains: classes, office days, commuting, social plans, exercise and occasions. Your wardrobe should reflect the activities that occupy most of your time.

<Callout title="Keep it personal" tone="note">
  There is no correct number of capsule pieces. A practical wardrobe in a warm climate will differ from one built around cold commutes or a formal workplace.
</Callout>

## Audit before buying

Pull out the clothes you already reach for. Record why they work—the fit, fabric, color or ease of care. Then separate genuine gaps from pieces that are simply unfamiliar or temporarily out of sight.

| Question | What it reveals |
| --- | --- |
| Can I wear it for a full day? | Comfort and fit |
| Does it work with three existing pieces? | Versatility |
| Can I care for it realistically? | Cost per wear |
| Does it suit my usual week? | Relevance |

## Choose a flexible palette

Pick two or three foundation colors that already appear in your wardrobe. Add one or two accents you genuinely enjoy. The purpose is easier coordination, not an entirely beige closet.

## Build with outfit roles

Instead of buying from a fixed shopping list, fill practical roles:

- dependable everyday bottoms;
- tops that work alone and beneath layers;
- one polished layer;
- comfortable everyday shoes;
- weather-specific outerwear;
- a small number of occasion pieces.

## Set a buying sequence

Prioritize the gap that prevents the most outfits. If several tops work only with one uncomfortable trouser, replace the trouser before buying another top.

## Test additions before removing tags

At home, try a new piece with at least three existing outfits. Check it while sitting, walking and layering. A theoretical match is not useful if the item is uncomfortable in practice.

<Divider />

## Let the capsule evolve

Review the wardrobe at the end of a season. Keep notes on what was repeatedly useful and what required effort. A good capsule changes with your life instead of becoming another rigid rule.
