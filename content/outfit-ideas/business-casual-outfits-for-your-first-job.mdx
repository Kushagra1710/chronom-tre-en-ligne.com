The best first-job wardrobe is not the one with the most clothes. It is the one that gives you a few dependable combinations for presentations, ordinary desk days and everything in between.

<Callout title="Before you shop" tone="tip">
  Check how people in your workplace actually dress. Business casual can mean a relaxed shirt and trousers in one office and a blazer-led uniform in another.
</Callout>

## The foundation pieces

Start with one dark trouser, one lighter trouser, two shirts, a fine knit and a clean jacket. Neutral colors make those pieces easier to repeat, while shoes, jewelry or a single accent color can keep the result personal.

## 1. Relaxed shirt and straight trousers

Choose a shirt with enough structure to look intentional, then soften it with straight or gently wide trousers. A partial tuck can define the waist without making the outfit feel overly formal.

## 2. Fine knit and tailored trousers

A lightweight crewneck or polo knit is useful when a shirt feels too formal. Keep the hem neat and let the trouser line create the polish.

## 3. Monochrome base with a contrasting layer

Build the outfit in one color family, such as charcoal and black, then add a taupe jacket or cardigan. The limited palette makes affordable separates feel more considered.

## 4. Simple dress with a structured jacket

Look for a dress that is comfortable when sitting and does not require constant adjustment. A clean jacket gives it office structure and can be removed later in the day.

## 5. Oxford shirt and dark denim

If denim is accepted, choose an even dark wash without distressing. Pair it with an Oxford-style shirt and refined shoes so the overall impression remains work-ready.

## 6. Column skirt and compact knit

A straight or softly A-line skirt works best when movement is easy. Combine it with a compact knit and keep accessories quiet for a balanced silhouette.

## 7. Unstructured blazer and a plain tee

A substantial, opaque T-shirt can sit comfortably beneath an unstructured blazer. Match it with tailored trousers rather than distressed denim to maintain the business-casual balance.

## 8. Matching separates worn as a set

Trousers and a jacket in the same fabric create an instant uniform. Wear them together on important days, then separate them across the rest of your wardrobe.

<Divider />

## A practical first-week rule

Begin slightly more polished than you think necessary, observe the office, and adjust. Fit, clean shoes and well-maintained fabric usually matter more than owning a large collection of formal pieces.
